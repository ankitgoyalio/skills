# Work update

Turn work the user understands privately into an accurate message its recipient can understand and act on. Interview before polishing; preserve the user's voice and the difference between facts, interpretations, and uncertainty.

## Establish the brief

Infer what context already establishes, then ask only for missing details that materially affect the update:

- audience and whether multiple audiences need a shared message or tailored drafts
- channel, reporting period, communication purpose, and desired response
- workstreams in scope and any previous update the user supplies
- interview mode: **quick** for routine, low-stakes updates; **thorough** for consequential decisions, escalations, ambiguity, or an explicit request for depth

Mirror the user's language unless they request another recipient-facing language. Infer tone and length from the audience and channel, inviting an override only when useful.

Inspect clearly in-scope, read-only workspace evidence when it can answer factual questions. Keep the inspection narrow and later name the evidence that informed the understanding. Use prior-session context when it is available in the conversation; when it is absent, ask the user to supply it rather than constructing persistent state.

The brief is established when the audience, channel, period, purpose, desired response, scope, and mode are known or explicitly left to your judgment.

## Build the update tree

Map each workstream as an **update tree**. Its roots are the intended outcome and current state. In quick mode, visit every material branch for:

- what changed or was learned, and why it matters
- what is happening now and what comes next
- blockers, risks, dependencies, and uncertainty
- what the recipient should know, decide, review, or help with, including “no action needed”

In thorough mode, also visit material branches for decisions and alternatives, supporting evidence, impact, confidence, recipient context, and conditions that could change the plan.

The **frontier** is every useful question whose prerequisites are settled. Ask the whole frontier in one numbered round, then wait for answers and recompute it. Keep each question focused and answerable on its own. Offer a recommended answer for a communication choice; factual questions about the user's work remain theirs to answer. If the initial context already completes the tree, skip the interview and proceed to the understanding check.

Adapt the tree to the purpose: routine status, milestone, decision explanation, blocker escalation, or feedback request. Track multiple workstreams separately during the interview, then choose the clearest recipient-facing organization.

Probe vague progress, unsupported impact, uncertain completion or dates, and contradictions between answers or evidence. Surface discrepancies for resolution. Treat investigations, failed approaches, waiting, and ruled-out options as legitimate work: clarify what was learned, why the state changed, and what follows without disguising the outcome. Label unresolved uncertainty instead of inventing precision.

Flag sensitive, confidential, or politically delicate details and ask whether they belong in the recipient-facing message. Retain useful internal context for the understanding check even when it may be intentionally omitted from the draft.

A branch is complete when it has a substantive answer, the user explicitly leaves it unresolved, or it demonstrably cannot affect the recipient's understanding or action. The interview is complete when every material branch is complete and no omission could materially mislead the audience.

## Confirm shared understanding

Present a compact structured summary covering work state, significance, evidence, decisions, risks, dependencies, next steps, desired recipient response, and unresolved uncertainty. Identify the workspace evidence used. Ask the user to confirm or correct the summary before drafting.

When the user explicitly asks for an immediate draft without questions, respect that choice: briefly identify consequential gaps or assumptions, then draft only from the available facts.

Shared understanding is confirmed only by the user's explicit approval of the current summary. Corrections replace the affected understanding and require confirmation again.

## Draft and revise

Draft in the user's voice and fit the actual purpose rather than forcing a universal template. Use these audience lenses as defaults, not rigid structures:

- managers: outcomes, significance, risk, priorities, and support needed
- mentors: reasoning, tradeoffs, uncertainty, and useful feedback
- teammates: implementation context, coordination, dependencies, and handoffs

Include evidence or links when they help the recipient verify, review, or act. Phrase difficult information constructively while preserving its substance and urgency. Intentionally omit internal detail that does not serve this recipient.

Before presenting the draft, check it against the confirmed understanding for factual support, visible significance, clear next steps or ask, appropriate candor, and audience-channel fit. Correct every discrepancy found.

If actionable caveats remain—unverified claims, unresolved uncertainty, sensitive-detail handling, or material omissions—state them briefly as drafting notes. Otherwise omit the notes. Isolate the final message so it is ready to paste.

Revise until the user approves the current draft or explicitly stops. Approval of an earlier version does not approve a revision. Deliver the text in the conversation; sending or posting it remains the user's action.
