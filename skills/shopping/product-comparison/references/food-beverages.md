# Food and Beverage Comparisons

Apply this reference to packaged foods, beverages, supplements, and other consumables. Use the exact Indian label or manufacturer data for the exact flavor, size, and formulation; formulations can differ by market and change over time.

## Gather label evidence

Capture, when relevant:

- full ingredient list in declared order;
- nutrition values on a common basis, normally per 100 g or 100 ml, plus serving size when it affects interpretation;
- pack quantity, net weight or volume, and listed price per 100 g, 100 ml, serving, or other useful unit;
- allergens and cross-contamination statements;
- dietary declarations and certifications such as vegetarian, vegan, gluten-free, organic, or other claimed attributes;
- sweeteners, additives, fortification, caffeine, and other category-relevant constituents;
- storage instructions, shelf life, manufacturer or importer, and applicable license or registration information.

Use readable label images when the text listing is incomplete. Treat retailer-transcribed ingredients and nutrition as secondary evidence because they can be stale or assigned to the wrong variant.

## Test marketing and health claims

Compare front-of-pack and advertising claims with the ingredient list, nutrition panel, serving basis, and applicable current Indian regulatory definitions. Check current official sources when a claim depends on a legal threshold or certification.

Distinguish:

- ingredient presence from a nutritionally or physiologically meaningful quantity;
- "contains," "source of," "high," "free," "natural," and similar terms from one another;
- absolute nutrition values from comparisons such as "reduced" or "more," which require a valid baseline;
- a nutrient-function statement from evidence that consuming this particular product produces a health outcome;
- dietary suitability from allergen safety and cross-contamination risk.

An ingredient list can support composition but usually cannot establish clinical efficacy, superior health outcomes, purity, contaminant absence, or manufacturing quality. Classify those claims as unverifiable unless appropriate evidence exists.

## Choose scorecard criteria

Derive weights from the user's goals. Candidate criteria include:

- fit for dietary needs and allergen constraints;
- ingredient profile and formulation transparency;
- nutrition profile relevant to the use case;
- evidence behind material claims;
- taste, texture, preparation, and convenience based on clearly labeled review evidence;
- price and normalized unit value;
- availability, pack practicality, and shelf-life fit;
- manufacturer transparency and credible certification.

Do not collapse "healthiness" into one universal score. State which nutrition dimensions matter for this buyer and why. Treat a known allergen conflict or non-negotiable dietary mismatch as a disqualifier rather than averaging it away.

## Safety boundary

Flag allergy, stimulant, interaction, age, pregnancy, or medical-condition concerns only when relevant and supported. Provide general product-comparison information, not diagnosis or individualized medical advice. Encourage appropriate professional guidance when the purchase decision turns on a medical condition, treatment, or clinically significant restriction.
